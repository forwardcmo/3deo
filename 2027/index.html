The one big idea

3DEO wins when the technical buyer understands Geiger-mode before they write the RFP.

Most prospects still spec projects in linear-mode language, and that quietly writes you out. Every pillar in this plan exists to get you into the conversation earlier: teach the market, show the data, and stay close to the accounts that matter until their funding trigger hits.

The four pillars are the what. The roadmap, budget, team rhythm and scorecard are the how. If you only read one section, read the last one.

The biggest gap isn't a tactic. It's the terminology gap.

How buyers write the RFP today

Linear-mode language

  • Minimum photon returns per pulse
  • Number of passes and overlap
  • Points per square meter written for low, slow flights
What 3DEO actually delivers

Geiger-mode outcomes

  • Single-photon sensitivity, so you fly higher, faster and wider
  • 4–6 viewpoints in one pass with agile geo-referenced scanning
  • Dense, low-shadow point clouds under canopy and in urban canyons

When the spec is written in the left column, Geiger-mode looks "non-compliant" before anyone talks to you. Closing that gap is the throughline of this plan.

Pillar 1

Demand generation and lead generation

When someone with a wide-area mapping problem goes looking, they find 3DEO and have a clear, low-risk next step. Four engines, and nothing else.

Engine 1 · Search

Own the problems, not just the product name

Hardly anyone searches "Geiger-mode lidar" yet. They search for the problem. Build pages and run Google Ads around the problems your systems solve best.

statewide lidar collectionwide area lidar mappinghigh altitude lidarwildfire fuel mapping lidarlidar under tree canopyvegetation management lidartransmission corridor lidarQL1 lidar3DEP lidar

$1,000–1,500 a month. Exact and phrase match only, geo-targeted to the US, Canada, the Nordics and Australia. Every ad lands on an industry page with the Request samples offer.

Engine 2 · LinkedIn

Your voice first, ads second

Your personal profile is the most valuable marketing channel 3DEO has. Buyers in this space trust people, not logos.

  • You: two posts a week. One technical insight, one point cloud with a sentence about what it shows. Your marketing lead drafts; you edit and post.
  • Company page: two posts a week, mostly reshares, news and "point cloud of the week."
  • Ads, $1,000–2,500 a month: geospatial program managers, GIS and remote sensing leads, forest inventory managers, utility vegetation managers, ISR program managers.
Engine 3 · Events

Fewer shows, more meetings

Geo Week is the anchor. Add two or three shows where Tier 1 accounts actually go: likely one defense (SPIE Defense + Commercial Sensing or GEOINT), one forestry or wildfire, one utilities. Confirm 2027 dates with Chris before committing.

Book meetings before you arrive. Three weeks of pre-show outreach, a live booth demo, a QR code to a HubSpot form, and a three-email follow-up within 48 hours.

Engine 4 · Signals

Catch the deals that never search

Government and large-utility work often starts in a tender portal. Set saved alerts on SAM.gov, CanadaBuys and provincial tender sites, plus Google Alerts for wildfire funding, statewide lidar programs and utility wildfire-mitigation plans.

When an alert hits, the marketing lead flags it in the Monday pipeline review.

A one-page map showing how many flight hours Sequoia needs to cover their entire jurisdiction at their spec, against a linear-mode approach. Built from your area collection charts.

The offer

A live walkthrough for their technical team of a point cloud from terrain that looks like theirs, with Brandon driving.

Primes

The teaming one-pager

Triggers

Trigger-based plays

When a utility files a wildfire mitigation plan or a province announces inventory funding, that account jumps to the front of the line.

Timing

Start 3–6 months ahead

Canadian provinces run an April–March fiscal year, so Oct–Jan sets priorities. US federal money moves in the summer before the Sept 30 year-end.

Pillar 4

Sales enablement and lifecycle marketing

This pillar makes you the most money soonest, because it works on relationships you already have. The rule we agreed on last summer: no dead ends. Every deal, past deal and customer always has a next step.

Build the enablement kit once

Q1 · mostly pulled from existing decks and proposals
Sell it
  • A master deck, plus short versions for forestry, defense and ISR, and utilities
  • One-pagers for Zion, Wrangell, Sequoia and Acadia
  • The Geiger vs. linear comparison chart, simplified for non-engineers
  • Sample datasets and viewer links by industry
Move it
  • A discovery guide built on your questions: Do they own a plane? Scene size and complexity? Their business model? Resolution or 3DEP quality level?
  • A pilot proposal template with scope, timeline and success criteria
  • A teaming one-pager for primes
  • "Explain it to your boss" one-pagers for general decision-makers
